- [ ] 250g Mascarpone
- [ ] 4 Egg Yolks
- [ ] 50g Sugar (1-2 tbsp per egg yolk)
- [ ] Strong Coffee
- [ ] Cocoa Powder (dark, unsweetened)
- [ ] Savoiardi Biscuits (~24 pieces or 200g pack)
- [ ] 150ml Heavy Cream (another 150g mascarpone works too)
- [ ] Frangelico
- [ ] Real Vanilla (optional but recommended)

Makes 4 large portions.

---

**Setup:** Take the eggs and cream out of the fridge comfortably ahead of time. Everything except the mascarpone should be at room temperature.

**Coffee:** Get this going first, as it needs some time to cool down. Proper espresso is obviously your first choice. Bialetti coffee works just as well, if not better (often less acidic). Bialetti tip: When coffee starts coming out of the spout, collect the first half in a separate container. You might not need all of the coffee. Splitting coffee into 2 batches gives you a stronger first batch. Also, use a tray as your container so the coffee cools down quicker. Avoid drip coffee, it's too watery.

**Eggs:** In a large bowl, separate the yolks from the whites. Add sugar. Save whites for breakfast.

**Cream:** Whip in a tall container until you see stiff peaks. Set aside. Skip this step if you only use mascarpone.

**Yolks & sugar:** Beat with the same electric hand mixer until foamy. The color of the egg yolks should change from dark to light yellow. Takes time, so be patient. "Uovo sbattuto" (beaten egg yolk with sugar) is the base of your custard.

**Mascarpone:** Add the cold mascarpone to the egg mixture. Whisk with hand mixer until smooth and creamy. Add the seeds from 1 vanilla bean. Always use real vanilla if you can; it goes a long way. Finish the custard by folding in the whipped cream with a spatula.

**Liquids:** Mix the coffee and Frangelico. If this is your first time making Frangelico-flavored Tiramisù, I'd start with a 2:1 ratio. Eventually, try 1:1 to maximize the hazelnut flavor. Many people use Amaretto, but in my view, it's too sweet and the hazelnut flavor from Frangelico pairs much better with coffee.

**Assembly:** Depending on the size of your dish, you may need to trim off the tips of the Savoiardi. Then, one by one, roll the biscuits in the coffee mixture and lay them out in your dish as tightly together as possible. The sugar side of the Savoiardi should face up. It acts like a moisture barrier between the biscuits and the custard. Don't be too modest with the liquid. The Savoiardi should not fall apart but should be fully soaked. Spread half of the custard on top and dust with cocoa powder. Dark Dutch-processed cocoa powder is your first choice. Avoid light brown cocoa powder. You need the bitterness from dark cocoa.

**Second layer:** Repeat the first layer, but leave out the cocoa powder. It would just get soggy while the Tiramisù rests. If you ever dare to make a 3-layer Tiramisù, please report your experience.

**Rest:** Cover the dish with foil and refrigerate overnight or for at least 6 hours. Cleaning the bowls is a treat on its own.

**Plating:** Dust the top layer with cocoa powder. The first slice is always a mess, so don't fret. Hide imperfections with more cocoa powder. If you want super clean slices, use dental floss to cut it.
